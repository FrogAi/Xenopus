# Reviewing images

## Full-zoom artifact review

Before presenting any image as finished, have fresh eyes inspect it at 100% (the `image-artifact-reviewer` agent when available; for a high-stakes image, also a second independent reviewer):

- Cut the image into overlapping crops at full scale covering every pixel, plus an overview and enlarged close-ups of every subject. Every crop gets examined; keep a coverage list. Inspect flat areas such as sky and open water a second time with raised contrast.
- Look for:
  - **Subjects:** extra, missing, merged or duplicated body parts and wrong species traits, including on small background people and animals; impossible poses; faces, hands, fingers and teeth that are malformed or uncanny; eyes with non-round pupils, or reflections and gaze that don't fit the scene; paired features that don't match (eyes, ears, earrings, glasses).
  - **Real things:** a specific product, vehicle or place that differs from its reference photos part by part (compare them, don't just judge plausibility); objects that couldn't work or be built, such as straps, wires, hinges, handles, mirrors, wipers or wheels that attach to nothing, merge or lack parts, stairs or railings that lead nowhere, or the wrong number of repeated parts (wheels, lug nuts, spokes, windows, legs); details wrong for the place or era (which side traffic drives on, road markings, signs, plate format, plants, buildings, clothing, technology).
  - **Scale, geometry and physics:** scale or proportions that contradict real dimensions or the neighbours (estimate from known sizes, such as a car's width against a lane); straight edges that wobble (rooflines, poles, lane lines, panel gaps); symmetric parts that differ (headlights, wheels, mirrors); lines that don't continue on both sides of something in front of them; perspective, horizon or vanishing lines that disagree, and a tilted horizon; floating objects and contact that doesn't touch; wind and gravity that disagree (flags, smoke, hair, foliage or water moving different ways, water that isn't level).
  - **Light and camera:** reflections, shadows and light that don't match, including harsh and soft light mixed in one scene; colour casts, white balance or grading that differ between areas lit the same way; different darkest blacks or brightest whites between parts; distant parts that don't grow hazier, paler and softer with distance; depth of field that changes where the distance doesn't; lens traits such as vignetting, colour fringing, distortion or motion blur in only some parts; an overall AI look (oversaturated colour, glowing or cinematic light a real camera wouldn't capture there, everything spotless and in showroom condition).
  - **Texture and detail:** melted, waxy, plastic, toy-like, painterly or watercolour texture; blur, softness or low detail that the target (for a photo, a real photograph at the delivery size) wouldn't have, across the whole image as well as in patches; detail or sharpness below the best areas at the same depth (a soft or upscaled region beside regenerated detail); banding, posterised gradients, compression blocks, noise or grain that differs between areas, clipped highlights and crushed shadows; faint low-contrast smudges, haze or blotches in flat areas (inspect them with raised contrast).
  - **Compositing:** cloned or repeated subjects or texture patches (the same glints, edges or specks recurring at a fixed offset); ghosts, smudges, seams, rectangles, soft bands, tone or sharpness steps, and broken continuity of edges, stems, ripples or reflections; cut-out edges with light or dark outlines, fringes in an old background's colour, edges sharper or softer than their distance allows, or no light from a bright background wrapping onto the subject's edge.
  - **Brief, text and rights:** anything that contradicts the brief, such as the season, time of day or a required subject; a subject whose prominence contradicts the brief, blending in at display, phone or thumbnail size when it should be noticed, or dominating when it should support; garbled text or stray lettering; readable logos, watermarks or brand text the brief didn't ask for; recognisable real people, including a generated face that resembles one, and readable personal details such as number plates, house numbers, names or screens; protected designs the brief didn't ask for (landmarks, distinctive buildings, artworks, characters, mascots, distinctive product designs or packaging); anything carried over from a reference photo, such as its background, framing, people, plates or watermark.
  - **Use:** a composition that fails its crops, where a subject or the space kept clear for text is cut off at the narrowest, widest, tallest or shortest viewport it's shown at, not just named breakpoints (each art-directed crop gets the full review); text over the image below 4.5:1 contrast (3:1 for large text), or a control's boundary below 3:1, against the lightest or darkest area behind it, at every crop and in each theme.
- Confirm each finding on a tighter crop before reporting it, with coordinates in the full image, severity and the crop path. Ignore choices the brief made on purpose.

Verify reported findings yourself, fix the confirmed ones at their source, rebuild, and re-inspect the fixed areas at full size. Show the user close-ups of what changed, from the actual master.

## Delivery copies

Review the copies the site actually serves, not only the master: every format, width and quality, fetched the way a browser fetches them.
- Open each at 100% beside the master, and again with raised contrast in flat areas: no banding that returned after export, blocks or ringing at high-contrast edges, fine texture smoothed away, colour bleeding along saturated red or blue edges, halos, jagged edges or moiré from resizing, and no colour or brightness shift from the master.
- Each is sRGB, converted with the profile embedded or untagged; a wide-gamut or other profile is never just relabelled.
- Each carries only metadata meant to be published: nothing from reference photos (camera, GPS, edit history) and no generator prompts or workflow, while wanted credit, licence and AI-source fields survive the optimizer; if an orientation tag is removed, its rotation is applied first.
- Every served width comes from the master without enlarging it, covers the largest display size at 2x within the page's size budget, and matches the width and height the page declares; a share image is about 1.91:1 (1200 by 630 is the common size) with nothing important near its edges.
- When the image could pass as a real photo of real people, places, objects or events, decide with the user whether it needs an AI label, and never present it as a documentary photo.

## Blind comparisons

When comparing versions or settings, crop the same areas at delivery scale, shuffle them, label them with letters, record your own ranking before revealing the key, and give the user the same blind sheet when their eye decides. Report both rankings and the key.
