# Show meaningful differences

Choose visuals around the reader's question: a chart for how profiles or results compare, a sequence for how adaptation changes over time, a before-and-after screenshot or clip for visible quality, or a table for choices and tradeoffs. When both quality and performance matter, show the output difference as well as the resource impact that can be supported; one does not prove the other. Match the publication's supported format; use a clear simpler representation or disclose a material tooling or evidence gap when necessary.

Verify the data, calculation and meaning of each comparison. A requested improvement is a premise to check, not a conclusion to manufacture. Label observed measurements, modeled estimates and illustrative scenarios in ordinary language beside the result. Preserve relevant units, conditions, applicability and uncertainty; use comparable baselines and honest scales. Media comparisons need matching scenes and presentation so different content, crops or playback settings do not masquerade as quality gains. Never invent measurements or present generated/reconstructed media as captured product evidence. When numbers are unavailable, show only supported qualitative differences and make any unmet requested comparison explicit.

Check that the delivered visual is legible in its intended context and agrees with the prose. Use direct labels, meaningful alternative text and a text explanation of essential information. Charts and screenshots must help readers understand, including when viewed on a small screen; decoration or redundant graphics do not satisfy this purpose.
